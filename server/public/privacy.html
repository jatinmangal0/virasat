Privacy

Last updated 5 November 2026

In short: you can walk the street, pick a city and watch Gaju without signing in, and then we keep nothing about you. When you sign in, Virasat keeps your name, email and profile photo, plus your stamps, scores and points, so they follow you to any device. We never see your Google password, we show no ads, we do no tracking, and we sell nothing. You can withdraw your consent by deleting your account, and everything in it, at any time.

For students

  • You do not need an account to explore. Just pick a city.
  • Joining your class? Use a nickname, like "Riya S", not your full name.
  • Your teacher sees your nickname, which cities you opened, your best scores and your stamps. Nothing else.
  • Under 18 and want your own account? A parent or guardian has to say yes first.
  • Want it all gone? Press Delete my account in your passport, or ask a grown-up to write to us.

The law we follow

We handle personal data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025. Many parts of these Rules come into force in stages up to May 2027; we follow them now anyway.

The basis for keeping your data is your consent, which you give when you sign in or join a class. We keep only what we need to run your passport, your stamps and your class, and we use it for nothing else.

What we keep, and why

If you only look around

Nothing about you goes into our database. Our web host's server logs see your IP address (see How long we keep it).

When you sign in

  • With Google: your name, email address, profile photo, and the account number Google gives us for this site. Google checks your password; we only receive a signed note from Google saying who you are. We ask for nothing else from your Google account.
  • With email and password: your name, email address, and your password in scrambled form (a one-way hash), so even we cannot read it.
  • Joining a class with a code: only the nickname you type. No email or phone number is needed.
  • With a phone number: for a few days during an early test (31 October to 1 November 2026) the site asked for a phone number. That sign-in is switched off. If you used it, we still hold that number with your account until you delete it.
  • When your account was made and when you last signed in.

While you learn

  • Which cities you opened and which parts you read.
  • Your quiz scores, your stamps and how well inked they are, and your points.
  • Which classes you joined, and any classes you teach.

How we use it

  • To keep your passport, stamps, scores and points, and show them to you on any device.
  • To greet you by your first name, for example when Gaju says "Namaste" or the site welcomes you back. Only you see this greeting.
  • To show your class's teacher how the class is doing (see Classes and teachers).
  • If you are on the editing team: to record which facts you wrote or checked.

We do not use your data for advertising, profiling, or any purpose not listed here.

Your consent, and taking it back

  • Giving it: the sign-in box tells you what we keep and links to this page. By pressing Sign in with Google, Create account, or Join for a class, you agree to us keeping that data for the reasons above. Nothing is kept before you press.
  • Taking it back: this is as easy as giving it. Sign in, open My passport, and press Delete my account. Or write to the email in Who is responsible.
  • What happens then: your account, progress, stamps, scores, points and class places are deleted straight away, and you are signed out. Anything we did with your data before that stays lawful, but we stop.
  • If you signed in with Google, you can also remove Virasat from your Google account settings, under "Third-party apps and services". That stops Google sign-in, but it does not delete your Virasat account, so delete that too.

Classes and teachers

For students

  • Use a nickname your teacher will recognise, like your first name and an initial ("Riya S"). Please do not type your full name, phone number or anything else about yourself.
  • A class account made with just a code and nickname stays signed in on that device for 30 days. After that it cannot be opened again, so make a full account if you want to keep your stamps longer (with a parent's consent if you are under 18).

What a teacher can see and do

  • Can see, for their own class only: each student's nickname, when they joined, how many times they opened each city, how many parts they read, their best quiz score and their stamps.
  • Can do: set a city as homework, with a due date.
  • Cannot see a student's email, phone number, photo, password or points, or anything from other classes.
  • Cannot change or delete a student's work or account.

If a teacher leaves

If the teacher deletes their account, the class goes with it: its code, its list of members and its homework are deleted. Students' own accounts and progress stay theirs.

Cookies and your browser

  • One cookie keeps you signed in. Page scripts cannot read it, it is only sent over a secure connection, and it ends after 30 days or when you log out.
  • Your browser's own storage remembers things on your device only, and they are never sent to us: which cities you have already seen, whether night mode is on, which flashcards you have learned, and your best score in each Explore game. Clearing your browser's site data removes them.
  • Sound is off until you turn it on, and the choice is not saved: it is off again on your next visit. If we ever save it, or add music, we will list it here.
  • We do not use advertising or tracking cookies, and we do not run any analytics.

Who else handles it, and where

A few services help run the site. They handle data only to do that job for us:

  • Neon stores the database, in Singapore.
  • Vercel hosts the website and runs our server code in Singapore. Pages may be delivered from the Vercel location nearest you. Like any web host, it keeps short-lived server logs that include your IP address.
  • Google runs "Sign in with Google", on servers in the United States and other countries. Google's sign-in code loads only when you open the sign-in box.
  • Cloudinary serves the city photos and artwork from servers mainly in the United States. Your browser asks it for each picture, so it sees your IP address.
  • OpenStreetMap supplies the street map on each city's Explore page, from servers in Europe. Your browser asks it for the map pieces, so it sees your IP address.
  • Wikimedia Commons supplies a few place photos, so it may see your IP address when you open them.

Our fonts are kept on our own server, so loading a page does not contact Google.

Some of this data is handled outside India. The law allows this, except to countries the Government of India restricts; none of the places above is restricted today. We do not sell or share your data with anyone else.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and progress: until you delete your account. [Accounts with no sign-in for 3 years: we email you, if we have your email, 48 hours before deleting them. To be confirmed and built.]
  • Server logs (IP address, page asked for, time): kept by our host for a short time, at most a few days, to keep the site running and safe. [Check the log retention the DPDP Rules ask for.]
  • Backups: our database host keeps a short rolling history so we can undo mistakes. Deleted accounts drop out of it within 7 days.
  • Editing team: the facts you wrote or checked stay on the site after you leave, without your name.

Keeping it safe, and breaches

  • Every page and request uses a secure (HTTPS) connection.
  • Passwords are stored only as a salted one-way hash. Sign-in tokens are stored only as a hash, so a copy of the database cannot be used to sign in as you.
  • Sign-in and sign-up are rate-limited to slow down guessing.
  • Only a small, named group of editors can open the editing tools, and they cannot see passwords.
  • The database is reachable only by our server and by the site's owners.

If something goes wrong: if your data is ever exposed, we will tell you without delay, by email if we have it and with a notice on the site, saying what happened, what it means for you and what you can do. We will also report it to the Data Protection Board of India as the law requires.

Your rights

  • See it: ask us for a summary of what we hold about you and who handled it.
  • Fix it: ask us to correct or complete it.
  • Delete it: do it yourself in your passport, or ask us.
  • Nominate someone: you can name a person to use these rights for you if you die or become unable to. Write to us with their name and email.
  • Complain: to us first, and then, if you are not happy with our answer, to the Data Protection Board of India.

Write from the email you signed in with, so we know it is you. A class account has no email: tell us the class code and nickname.

Questions and complaints

Write to our grievance contact at [site email]. We will reply within 7 days and settle it within 30 days at most. If we cannot settle it, or you are not happy with the answer, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Changes to this page

If we change what we keep or how we use it, we will update this page and the date at the top. For a big change, we will show a notice on the site for at least 30 days and email everyone whose account has an email, before the change starts. If it needs new consent, we will ask for it.
